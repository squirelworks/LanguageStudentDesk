tlhIngan Hol

Klingon Language Teacher — Focus on the pIqaD Script

Qapla'! Welcome, warrior.

This app teaches tlhIngan Hol (the Klingon language) with a special emphasis on the pIqaD script — the proper writing system, not just the Latin romanization used by most resources.

Most apps stop at the Latin transcription. Here you learn to recognize, read, type and hand-write the real characters.

Why pIqaD matters

  • Authentic representation of the language
  • One-to-one sound-to-symbol mapping: 26 letters, one per sound
  • The form used by the Klingon Language Institute (KLI)

How pIqaD works on computers

pIqaD is not in the official Unicode standard. Everyone uses the ConScript Unicode Registry assignment in the Private Use Area (U+F8D0–U+F8FF), so pIqaD text only displays when a pIqaD font is installed. This app ships the open-licensed pIqaD qolqoS font, so everything here displays without installing anything. Text you copy from the Type in pIqaD tab needs a pIqaD font wherever you paste it.

Start with the alphabet, then practise typing and handwriting, then move to vocabulary and basic grammar (note the OVS word order).

Type in pIqaD

Handwriting

Trace the faint letter with your mouse, finger or pen, then turn the guide off and write it from memory. pIqaD has no official stroke order; a common approach is the long outer strokes first, then the short inner ones, top to bottom.

Numbers

pIqaD has its own digits, written in base ten like ours. Spoken numbers add maH (ten), vatlh (hundred) and SaD or SanID (thousand) after the digit: wejmaH loS is 34.

Basic Grammar

Word Order: OVS

Klingon is Object-Verb-Subject. The object comes first, then the verb, then the subject.

Example: Duj legh HoD = "The captain sees the ship" (literally: ship sees captain)

This is a clean OVS sentence: object (Duj "ship"), verb (legh "sees"), subject (HoD "captain").

Equative sentences ("to be") are a special case. In tlhIngan jIH = "I am a Klingon", the subject pronoun is fused with the verb, so it is glossed "Klingon I-am".

Verb Prefixes

Verbs take prefixes that indicate the subject and object. For simple sentences:

  • jI- = I (no object): jIyaj "I understand"
  • bI- = you (no object): bIyaj "you understand"
  • vI- = I (object is him/her/it): vIlegh "I see it"

Useful Phrases

  • nuqneH — What do you want? (the Klingon greeting)
  • Qapla' — Success!
  • maj — Good.
  • ghobe' — No.
  • HIja' — Yes.
  • jIyajbe' — I don't understand.
